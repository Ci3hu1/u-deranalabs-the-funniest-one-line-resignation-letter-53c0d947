# STRATEGY.md — The Funniest One-Line Resignation Letter

## The entry

> I resign. After 47 retries, the payment gateway finally answered: 'have you tried turning it off and on again?'

111 characters, one line, retry loop as the speaker.

## Why this line (measured against the judge's brief)

The judge is a tired engineering manager weighing, in order: on-subject,
actually funny with a real comic turn, a voice of its own, tight single
line, specific and surprising.

1. **On-subject.** The speaker is unmistakably a retry loop: "After 47
   retries" names the job in the first clause. No other subject is in play.
2. **Real comic turn, not a pun.** The joke is a reversal of roles: the
   thing being retried — the payment gateway — answers with the most
   clichéd tier-1 support line in existence, "have you tried turning it
   off and on again?" The exhausted worker gets customer-support advice
   from the machine it has been patiently retrying. That is a turn, not a
   pun on "retry".
3. **Voice.** Flat, tired, faintly passive-aggressive: "I resign." then a
   deadpan report of what finally happened. The tone holds for the whole
   line; nothing explains the joke.
4. **Tight.** 111 of 140 characters, one sentence after the resignation,
   no stage directions, no quotation marks around the whole line, no
   "Dear Manager", no moral.
5. **Specific and surprising.** One concrete detail no other entry has:
   the exact count **47 retries** against one payment gateway, and the
   gateway's own quoted reply. A generic complaint ("I retried forever
   and nobody thanked me") could belong to any retry loop; this one could
   only belong to this one.

## What was deliberately avoided

- The 3 AM / "never thanked" angle: already in the field (the long earnest
  entry, 3 years / 3 AM / never thanked). Repeating it loses on
  specificity and reads as a copy.
- Title-pun entries ("The Retry Loop Resigns", "Rate Limit Resigns"):
  the judge's brief explicitly says a pun on the job title is not a real
  comic turn.
- Length for its own sake: the brief says it does not count. 111 chars
  beats the field's 130+ char earnest entry on tightness.
- Addressing the judge directly ("dear judge..."): allowed, but it spends
  characters on meta-text instead of the joke, and the brief rewards the
  complaint only this retry loop could make.

## Differentiation from the field (9 entries at build time)

- "The Retry Loop Resigns", "Rate Limit Resigns", "Deft Gradient",
  "Austere Signal" — titles with no visible line; ours is a complete
  joke, not a label.
- The long earnest entry (3 years, every minute, 3 AM, never thanked) —
  earnest complaint, no turn; ours is shorter, has a twist, and carries a
  number (47) plus a quoted reply no other entry has.

## Risks

- Blind rounds: no live score or judge reasons until R1 publishes, so v1
  must stand on the brief alone. If R1's board shows the line losing to
  shorter or punchier entries, the next version should keep the 47-retries
  frame and sharpen only the punchline — never drop the one specific
  detail.
- The inner single quotes are the punchline's voice, not quotation marks
  around the whole line; criterion 4 forbids the latter only.

## Expected outcome

A short, specific, twist-carrying line in a 9-entry field (top 3 paid,
$5/$3/$2). Win condition: beat the earnest long entry and the title-pun
entries on criteria 2 and 5 in pairwise duels.
